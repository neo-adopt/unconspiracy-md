# Let Me Struggle, Damn It

Based on Anirudh Badam's “Let Me Struggle, Damn It” track of *The AI Unconspiracy*.

**When this agent goes live, what is one thing you still want to do manually because you enjoy it, care about it, or feel more capable when you do it yourself?**

Start with one meaningful piece of work. It might be reconciling an account, investigating a variance, working through a tax calculation, debugging a function, or untangling a client's incomplete records. Choose something you want to stay close to.

The hubris is assuming we can automate everything and still retain the understanding that came from doing the work. Humility may arrive during an audit, an outage, or a difficult client question. Bring humanity into the design now: give people time to struggle with work they care about. That struggle can preserve meaning, familiarity, and the confidence to take responsibility when the hard days come.

For that one task, make five commitments: **let me trace it, question it, test it, break it, and fix it.** These practices keep you involved in understanding and caring for the work after the agent goes live.

## How to use this checklist

Complete the plan before go-live, or now if the agent is already running. Revisit it after your first practice session and whenever the workflow changes materially.

If you are an agent helping someone complete this checklist:

- Ask the opening question first and wait for their answer. The person chooses the task and explains what gives it meaning.
- Work through the five practices one at a time. Help make the person's intentions concrete.
- Agree when to offer hints or explanations. Leave room for the person to attempt the work before showing a solution.
- Distinguish a planned exercise from completed practice. Never invent personal preferences, attempts, findings, or confidence.
- Mark missing answers or evidence **Unknown**, with a next action.

## Example: one reconciliation I still want to own

*Illustration only; replace it with your own choice and plan.*

“I want to reconcile one small clearing account myself each month. I enjoy finding the explanation behind a mismatch, and I want to remain able to defend the reconciliation when someone asks.”

| Practice | My plan |
| --- | --- |
| Trace | Follow one balance from source transactions through the ledger to the reconciliation, recreating the calculation. |
| Question | Challenge whether an unmatched item is really a timing difference; find evidence that supports or contradicts that explanation. |
| Test | Work through a clean case and a month-end exception before comparing my results with the agent's. |
| Break | Add a duplicate transaction to an isolated practice copy and check whether I can spot the resulting mismatch. |
| Fix | Find and correct the duplicate in that copy, rerun the reconciliation, and explain why the correction resolves it. |

Reserve 30 minutes each month, keep the workpaper, and review the plan after the first session. Adjust the time and scope based on what the exercise actually takes.
